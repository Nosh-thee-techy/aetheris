Verification layer · flood data · Kenya

Rain data said “flood” in seven places. Radar could confirm one.

Flood payouts and alerts rest on satellite rainfall estimates that are sparse on the ground and often wrong in heavy rain. Aetheris checks them against Sentinel-1 radar, which sees whether land is actually under water, for each place and week, and records the result where anyone can check it or challenge it. This is the first week we checked: the Nyando basin, 4–6 May 2024.

01

When the data misses the flood, nobody can see why

Smallholder farmers and pastoralists in flood-prone basins are paid by weather-index insurance, or helped by alerts, only when the data says a flood happened. When that data misses their flood they get nothing, and the decision was made by a single operator, from an estimate, with no reason attached.

Aetheris attaches the reason. Each decision shows what each source measured, against which line, and who signed. It also counts how often the data misses real floods, so triggers can be designed more fairly over time. Today the benefit to farmers is indirect, and every payout in this demo is on a testnet or a sandbox.

What the research says

  • Ground stations are few. Africa’s average density of stations meeting WMO standards is eight times below the recommended level, and as of October 2024 Chad was reporting 3% of the surface observations it is required to. Eos
  • Satellite rainfall is imperfect where it matters. Against 596 TAHMO gauges, every product tested overestimated light rain and underestimated heavy rain (preprint). In East Africa, CHIRPS had a 28% wet bias in daily rainfall (published version). Another study found CHIRPS missed 98% of the daily extreme-rain events its gauges recorded (36 stations). The studies differ on how good CHIRPS is, so read the numbers with care.
  • Ground data is gated. ICPAC receives station data from national met services under bilateral agreements, and each service sets its own terms, some with cost recovery. ICPAC
  • Index insurance already runs in Kenya on satellite triggers. In 2021 Etherisc and ACRE Africa onboarded more than 17,000 smallholders and began paying some through M-Pesa. Etherisc Those sources do not measure how often such triggers miss a real flood. That is what this project counts.

What is new here, and what is not. We found no published open-code method that cross-validates gauge readings against satellite products. We did not search specifically for radar-versus-rainfall cross-checks, which may exist in the flood-mapping literature, so we do not claim the cross-check itself is new. What we add is recording each disagreement as a signed, challengeable public claim tied to a payout rule.

02

A flood is accepted only when radar and a second source both sign

Each place and week is one claim. Signing means “this payout rule fired”, not “I saw something”. Silence counts as no.

  1. Radar

    Radar signs

    Sentinel-1 shows open water on enough of the place’s land, with permanent water removed. Required. Without it nothing opens.

  2. Rainfall

    A second source signs

    Rain over the upstream catchment crosses the line. Rainfall alone can never open a claim. Each signer posts a 0.01 ETH bond and its own evidence hash.

  3. Anyone

    Anyone can challenge

    For 24 hours, anyone can stake 0.01 ETH against the claim. A challenge freezes it. The referee can only uphold or reject.

  4. Contract

    It finalizes

    If no one challenged, or the referee stayed silent for 48 hours, the claim stands. Bonds come back. A rejected claim slashes the bonds to the challenger.

  5. Escrow

    It pays once

    Only a finalized claim releases funds, only to beneficiaries committed before the season, and each place-week-rule can be paid at most once.

The rule is frozen. Radar line: open water below -16 dB on at least 5.0% of land pixels. Rainfall line: 40 mm over three days on the upstream catchment, lag 0 days. Fixed on 2026-10-12, before this week was scored, and hashed into every claim id. These are untuned starting values, not fitted to this flood. Check the hash against the contract.

03

Who this is for, and how we know

The direct user is the trigger analyst at an insurer or aggregator: the person who decides what counts as a flood, owns the basis-risk problem, and can use a disagreement record. The people most affected, smallholders and pastoralists, are the beneficiaries, not the users. Data producers and researchers are the community we hope to help, not customers.

How it is built, and how much of it exists

Data goes in on the left and a payout rule sits on the right. Each part says what state it is really in.

  1. Sources

    Sentinel-1 radar (RTC), CHIRPS rainfall, JRC permanent water.

    Read by hand for one week

  2. Frozen rule

    config/rule.yaml. Its hash is part of every claim id.

    Frozen, hash on the testnet

  3. Attester jobs

    Radar and rainfall jobs decide sign or stay silent from the rule. Each signs with its own key and bond.

    Decisions written. The demo claim was submitted by the deploy script with the same keys

  4. FloodTrigger

    Holds claims, bonds, the challenge window and the referee’s only two choices.

    Deployed on Sepolia. Unaudited

  5. PayoutEscrow

    Pays a finalized claim once to a table committed in advance.

    Deployed on Sepolia. Beneficiaries are placeholders

  6. M-PESA mirror

    Copies a release into a record labelled SANDBOX.

    A function that writes a record. No Daraja call is made

Not built: a committee or vote for the referee (one named key decides today), a ground-gauge attester, IMERG rainfall, and any check that real people want this.

05

What we are not claiming

This is not ground-truth validation, and it does not fix flood insurance.

  • Radar has blind spots.It sees open water. It does not see flood under crops, trees or buildings, and Sentinel-1’s 2024 revisit is twelve days, so a flood can rise and fall between passes.
  • The sources overlap.Rainfall and radar describe the same storm. Two signatures are not two independent witnesses, and the agreement table is how we show how much they add.
  • Satellite rainfall is an estimate.CHIRPS tends to overestimate light rain and underestimate heavy rain. Gauges are scarce here. IMERG is not connected.
  • The referee is one key.In this demo a single named key can uphold or reject a challenge. A committee, then a vote, is the intended path. The contract has one function for it, so swapping does not change the rest.
  • One season, seven news reports.Counts here are indicative. Reports favour places that reporters and rescuers could reach. There are no “did not flood” labels yet.
  • Payouts are not real.The escrow holds testnet ETH. M-PESA is a Daraja sandbox mirror that moves no money. The contracts are unaudited.
