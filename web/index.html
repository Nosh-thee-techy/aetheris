Verification layer · flood data · Kenya

Rain data said “flood” in 7 places. Radar could confirm 1.

Flood payouts and alerts rest on satellite rainfall estimates that are sparse on the ground and often wrong in heavy rain. Aetheris checks them against Sentinel-1 radar, which sees whether land is actually under water, for each place and week, and records the result where anyone can check it or challenge it. This is the first week we checked: the Nyando basin, 4–6 May 2024.

01

When the data misses the flood, nobody can see why

Smallholder farmers and pastoralists in flood-prone basins are paid by weather-index insurance, or helped by alerts, only when the data says a flood happened. When that data misses their flood they get nothing, and the decision was made by a single operator, from an estimate, with no reason attached.

Aetheris attaches the reason. Each decision shows what each source measured, against which line, and who signed. It also counts how often the data misses real floods, so triggers can be designed more fairly over time. Today the benefit to farmers is indirect, and every payout in this demo is on a testnet or a sandbox.

02

A flood is accepted only when radar and a second source both sign

Each place and week is one claim. Signing means “this payout rule fired”, not “I saw something”. Silence counts as no.

  1. Radar

    Radar signs

    Sentinel-1 shows open water on enough of the place’s land, with permanent water removed. Required. Without it nothing opens.

  2. Rainfall

    A second source signs

    Rain over the upstream catchment crosses the line. Rainfall alone can never open a claim. Each signer posts a 0.01 ETH bond and its own evidence hash.

  3. Anyone

    Anyone can challenge

    For 24 hours, anyone can stake 0.01 ETH against the claim. A challenge freezes it. The referee can only uphold or reject.

  4. Contract

    It finalizes

    If no one challenged, or the referee stayed silent for 48 hours, the claim stands. Bonds come back. A rejected claim slashes the bonds to the challenger.

  5. Escrow

    It pays once

    Only a finalized claim releases funds, only to beneficiaries committed before the season, and each place-week-rule can be paid at most once.

The rule is frozen. Radar line: open water below -16 dB on at least 5.0% of land pixels. Rainfall line: 40 mm over three days on the upstream catchment, lag 0 days. Fixed on 2026-10-12, before this week was scored, and hashed into every claim id. These are untuned starting values, not fitted to this flood.

Testnet deployment

The contracts are written and their tests run in CI, but nothing from this project is on a public testnet yet. Deploying needs a little test ETH in the project’s own test wallets, which has to come from a faucet. Until that happens this page shows no on-chain state, because inventing some would be the opposite of what it is for.

06

What we are not claiming

This is not ground-truth validation, and it does not fix flood insurance.

  • Radar has blind spots.It sees open water. It does not see flood under crops, trees or buildings, and Sentinel-1’s 2024 revisit is twelve days, so a flood can rise and fall between passes.
  • The sources overlap.Rainfall and radar describe the same storm. Two signatures are not two independent witnesses, and the agreement table is how we show how much they add.
  • Satellite rainfall is an estimate.CHIRPS tends to overestimate light rain and underestimate heavy rain. Gauges are scarce here. IMERG is not connected.
  • The referee is one key.In this demo a single named key can uphold or reject a challenge. A committee, then a vote, is the intended path. The contract has one function for it, so swapping does not change the rest.
  • One season, seven news reports.Counts here are indicative. Reports favour places that reporters and rescuers could reach. There are no “did not flood” labels yet.
  • Payouts are not real.The escrow holds testnet ETH. M-PESA is a Daraja sandbox mirror that moves no money. The contracts are unaudited.
07

Who this is for

Working assumptions. Not yet validated with users.

Insurers and aggregators

They set and defend flood triggers and carry the cost of disputes. A checkable record of what each source said, and how often triggers miss, is something they can argue from.

County teams and humanitarian agencies

A second signal, from a different instrument, before funds or alerts go out.

ICPAC, Kenya Met and researchers

An open, repeatable check on how reliable satellite rainfall is where gauges are scarce.

Smallholder farmers and pastoralists

The people most affected. The benefit to them today is indirect: a visible reason for each decision, and a measure of how often the data misses.
